SOLDERING FIELD GUIDE

Learn soldering quality

Visual clues for common joint problems and a practical starting technique.

WHAT TO CHECK

Problem solder joint

Look for solder that has not wetted both the component lead and pad, a weak connection with too little solder, or a large irregular buildup. Solder that bridges neighboring pads can create an unintended electrical connection.

Appearance alone is not a complete electrical test. Check the joint against the component and board requirements.

A HEALTHY JOINT

Good soldering

A sound through-hole joint typically forms a smooth, continuous fillet between the pad and lead, with enough solder to wet both surfaces and no bridge to nearby connections.

The ideal shape depends on the component, pad design, solder alloy, and process. Use the assembly requirements when available.

A SIMPLE THROUGH-HOLE METHOD

How to make a cleaner joint

  1. 1
    Prepare safely

    Work with the board unpowered, secure it, use eye protection and good ventilation, and keep the hot iron in its stand.

  2. 2
    Heat both surfaces

    Place the iron tip so it touches the pad and component lead at the same time. Let both warm evenly.

  3. 3
    Feed solder into the joint

    Touch solder to the heated lead and pad, not just to the iron. Add only enough for a smooth fillet.

  4. 4
    Remove and let it cool

    Pull the solder away, then the iron. Keep the component still while the joint solidifies.

  5. 5
    Inspect the connection

    Look for complete wetting, a neat shape, and no solder bridges. Check continuity when appropriate.
